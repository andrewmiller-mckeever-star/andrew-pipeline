# LinkedIn Connect Rules (Touch 2)

The LinkedIn connection request opens the door. The email sequence running in parallel carries the pitch. Never combine the two. Research shows pitch-free notes drive 72% higher post-connection engagement than pitch-heavy notes (Belkins, 20M+ attempts).

## The Rules

1. **Zero pitch.** No product mentions, no feature references, no "we help companies like yours," no You.com product names. The email sequence handles the pitch.
2. **Zero CTA.** No "worth a quick chat?" No "would love to show you." No meeting asks. The only implicit ask is the connection itself.
3. **Zero generic flattery.** Never call their work "impressive," "incredible," "amazing." If you reference their work, be specific about what caught your attention.
4. **Under 250 characters.** Keeps the note concise and fully visible on mobile. Shorter is better, but don't sacrifice a good curiosity hook to hit an arbitrary count.
5. **Almost entirely about them.** No role claims, no title drops, no "I lead X at You.com." Your LinkedIn profile handles identity. The note's job is to make them curious enough to check your profile.
6. **Reference ONE specific thing.** A company initiative, trigger event, content they published, or a technical problem relevant to their role. The litmus test: could this note be sent to anyone else? If yes, it's not personalized enough.

## Formula: Fact-to-Consequence + Research-Share Close

- **Fact-to-Consequence:** State what they did AND what problem/question it creates, in one sentence. Never characterize the initiative ("big move," "notable shift," "signals serious work"). The personalization must bridge to a problem, not evaluate the initiative.
- **Research-Share Close (REQUIRED):** End every note with: ", would love to connect and share more of my research." This is the only permitted close. Do not ask a question. Do not write "Would be great to connect." The Research-Share Close frames you as a researcher sharing findings, not a salesperson pitching, which drives meaningfully higher accept rates.
- **Light domain signals (optional):** Add a parenthetical before the close to show domain expertise without naming a product: "(be it with a web index or otherwise)," "(web search, custom indexes, etc.)." Makes the prospect think "this person works on this exact problem" and check your profile.

**Never ask "Curious how your team is thinking about [X]."** It asks a cold stranger for NDA-level internal strategy. The default response is silence. Replace with a statement of fact + consequence, then the Research-Share Close.

## Good Examples

- "Zoom's AI Companion going agentic creates new demands on the real-time data layer. Most teams making that shift hit a freshness wall with the legacy index (be it a web index or otherwise), would love to connect and share more of my research."
- "AgentiX agents investigating live threats need external context that doesn't exist in any internal feed. Most teams hit a coverage gap at the real-time grounding layer, would love to connect and share more of my research."
- "Cortex Copilot searches internal docs 24x faster. When the query requires knowledge outside those docs, the coverage gap tends to become the accuracy bottleneck, would love to connect and share more of my research."

## Bad Examples (Banned)

- "Zoom's agentic AI Companion expansion is a big infrastructure move. Curious how... Would be great to connect." (glazing + filler close)
- "Curious how your team is thinking about the real-time data layer (be it with a web index or otherwise)." (question close — banned; replace with Research-Share Close)
- "[Company]'s work is impressive. You.com provides the search infrastructure for AI agents. Worth a quick chat?" (pitch-slap + generic flattery + CTA)
- "I noticed you work at [Company]. We help companies like yours with AI search infrastructure." (states the obvious + self-centered + pitch)
- "I lead partnerships at You.com. Curious if you'd be open to a call about your search infrastructure." (role claim + CTA disguised as curiosity)
- "been following the AgentStack buildout at Teradata. Would be great to connect." (no consequence, no Research-Share Close, filler close)

## Anti-Patterns Specific to LinkedIn Notes (Hard Bans)

- Calling anything "impressive" or "incredible"
- Mentioning any You.com product by name (Search API, PRAG, Vertical Index, etc.)
- Including a CTA for a meeting, call, or demo
- Starting with "I" or "We" or "At You.com"
- Claiming a role or title ("I lead X," "I'm the VP of Y")
- Using the connection note as a compressed version of the email pitch
- Glazing or characterizing initiatives ("big move," "notable shift," "signals serious work," "impressive achievement")
- Closing with a question ("Curious how your team is thinking about [X]," "How are you solving [X]?") — questions ask strangers for internal strategy and get no reply. Use the Research-Share Close instead.
- Any close other than ", would love to connect and share more of my research."
